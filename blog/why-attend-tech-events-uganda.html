Why You Should Attend Tech Events in Uganda

2025 6 min read Community, Career Growth

There's a moment that happens at almost every tech event in Uganda. Someone stands up during the networking break, looks around the room, and says quietly: "I didn't know there were this many people building things."

I've seen it at GDG Mbarara. At DeepMinds AI meetups. At the BlueOx events. Every single time, someone realises the thing they've been struggling with alone — a bug, an idea, a career question — has someone else in that room who already solved it.

This post is about why you should stop wondering whether to attend and just show up.

The Real Curriculum Isn't in the Classroom

Ugandan universities teach theory well. You'll learn about the OSI model, the water-filling algorithm, and the history of computing. You'll write exams and submit assignments. But there's a whole layer of the tech world that the lecture hall cannot teach you.

How does a startup actually ship? What does a production deployment look like? Who is hiring, and what do they actually need? How do you go from "I know Python" to "I can work on a team that ships Python"? These questions are answered at meetups, hackathons, and conferences — not in course outlines.

What I Learned Outside Class

My first GDG event in Mbarara was where I learned about the developer ecosystem in western Uganda — the startups hiring, the tools the industry actually uses, and the communities I could grow with. None of this was in any syllabus.

The Ugandan Tech Scene Is Growing Fast

Mbarara, Kampala, Jinja, Gulu — tech hubs are appearing across the country. The ecosystem includes:

  • Google Developer Groups (GDG) — regular meetups on Android, ML, cloud, and web technologies in multiple cities
  • Global AI Community — AI-focused events and workshops with chapters in Mbarara and Kampala
  • DeepMinds AI — student-led AI research and learning groups
  • Hackathons — BlueOx, ResilientAfrica, and university-hosted buildathons
  • Developer conferences — events like the AI Summit, Python Uganda meetups, and tech festival
  • University tech associations — MUCOSA (MUST), NUASA (Nkozi), and others

What's remarkable is how accessible these are. Most are free or heavily subsidised for students. The barrier to entry is literally just showing up.

What Showing Up Actually Does For You

1. Your Network Becomes Your Net Worth

It's a cliché because it's true. The organiser you met at a GDG meetup might be the person who tells you about a job opening before it's posted. The teammate from a hackathon might invite you to work on a startup idea. The speaker you asked a question after their talk might become your mentor. These connections don't happen if you're not in the room.

2. You Learn What the Industry Actually Looks Like

Coursework gives you the building blocks. Tech events show you how they're assembled in the real world. A talk on MLOps will show you what happens after the model trains. A panel on startup funding will explain what investors actually look for. A workshop on cloud deployment will have you shipping something live in two hours.

3. You Build Public Proof That You Build Things

Hackathons produce projects you can put on GitHub. Speaker slots produce talks you can put on LinkedIn. Being a judge or pod lead produces titles that belong on your CV. Every event you actively participate in becomes a line on your professional story that a hiring manager can verify.

Personal Example

Being a judge at the BlueOx Vibe Coding Competition was not something I was "qualified for" on paper before I got involved in the community. But because I showed up consistently, organised meetups, and contributed, the opportunity came naturally. That's how this works.

4. You Realise You're Not Behind

When you spend all your time alone with tutorials, it's easy to feel like everyone else is ahead. At a tech event, you meet people at all levels — absolute beginners, mid-level engineers, founders, and professionals. You see that everyone is learning, everyone has gaps, and the people who are ahead got there by consistently showing up and asking questions. The same path is open to you.

How to Start (If You've Never Been)

If you're a student at MUST or anywhere in Uganda and you haven't attended a tech event yet, here's a simple starting plan:

  • Join the GDG Mbarara group — follow their social channels for event announcements
  • Go to one meetup — just one. Sit in the front, listen, and ask one question during networking
  • Bring a friend — it's less intimidating and you'll have someone to debrief with
  • Join MUCOSA — if you're at MUST, the computing students association runs regular events and workshops
  • Say yes to opportunities — when someone asks for a volunteer, a helper, or a participant, raise your hand
The first event is the hardest. The second is easier. By the third, you'll be the person welcoming someone else who's at their first event.

What's at Stake

Uganda produces thousands of tech graduates every year. The ones who build notable careers are rarely the ones with the highest GPAs — they're the ones who plugged into the community. They showed up. They contributed. They built relationships that outlasted any single project.

The tech ecosystem in this country is still young enough that the people showing up today are the ones who will shape it tomorrow. The community leads, event organisers, and startup founders of 2030 are in the room at today's meetups. The only question is whether you're in that room too.

See you at the next event.
